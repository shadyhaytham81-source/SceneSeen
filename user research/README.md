# SceneSeen user research

Research with ESLSCA students (media and cinema students, and students in general) to understand how people watch
series and films, what they do when they like something on screen, and how they'd use SceneSeen. Participants can
also opt in to beta testing and to updates about the app.

## What we want to learn

Each question checks an assumption in the SRS:

| Question | Assumption it tests |
|---|---|
| Where and how do people watch: TV, Shahid / Watch It on a laptop, phone, clips on social media? | Camera scanning is the typical case |
| When they like something on screen, what do they do today: screenshot, Google Lens, ask in comments, forget it? | The problem on slide 2 is real |
| Do they want the exact item, or something similar at their budget? | Similar-first is useful enough |
| Would they tag the show, vote, or answer other people's requests? | The community loop will happen |
| App scan vs a QR code on screen: which feels natural, which feels intrusive? | Which comes first; what to pitch to platforms |

## Studies, in order

### 1. Survey (week 1, target 100+ responses)
- About 12 questions, ~5 minutes. See `survey.md`.
- Ends with three **separate** opt-ins: interviews, beta testing, updates about the app and its social accounts.
- Distribute through class groups, student clubs, and the media and cinema department.
- Output: watching-habit numbers for the SRS, plus the recruitment pool for everything below.

### 2. Interviews (weeks 1–2, 8–12 people)
- 20–30 minutes. Ask them to walk through the **last time** they noticed something they liked in a show. Real recent
  stories, not "would you use…" questions.
- Mix regular viewers with 3–4 media and cinema students. The students can speak to how styling and set design work,
  and how viewers notice them.

### 3. Concept comparison: app scan vs QR code (week 3, 15–20 people)
- Play an official YouTube clip on a TV or laptop.
- **Version A:** they open the app and point the camera at the screen.
- **Version B:** the same clip with a mock QR code overlaid.
- Measure time to reach a product, preference, and how intrusive each feels (1–5).
- Half the participants try A first and half try B first, so order doesn't decide the winner.

### 4. Usability tests with the real app (later in the semester, 20+ people)
- Tasks: scan a scene, open a product, save a look, answer a request.
- Then the System Usability Scale (SUS) questionnaire. This is grad objective 5 (SUS ≥ 70, 20+ users).

## Media and cinema students: beyond interviews
- **Test set:** photograph screens at real angles and lighting, and rate how similar products are to the garment
  (0 = not, 1 = somewhat, 2 = very). A trained eye for costume makes them better raters.
- **First spotters:** seed community answers with people who know styling.
- **Introductions:** some may know people in production or costume departments.

## Before starting
- **Ethics approval:** ask Dr. Mohammed Tharwat whether the university requires it for studies with people,
  especially since results go in the grad report.
- **Consent:** `consent_form.md`. Taking part in research never counts as agreeing to marketing; the three opt-ins
  stay separate.
- **Collect little:** no ID numbers. Store names and contact details apart from answers; nothing identifying in the
  report.
- **Reporting:** patterns and counts ("9 of 12 watch Ramadan series on TV"), never individual people.
- **Language:** the drafts are in English. Add an Arabic version if responses are thin.
