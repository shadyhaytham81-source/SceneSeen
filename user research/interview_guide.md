# Interview guide

20–30 minutes, one participant, two team members: one asks, one takes notes. In person or on a call.
Use the full consent form (`consent_form.md`) before starting.

## Before the interview
- Recruit from the survey's "happy to do an interview" opt-ins. Aim for 8–12 people:
  - 3–4 media and cinema students;
  - the rest regular viewers, mixing people who watch mostly on TV, on a laptop, on a phone and through clips.
- Check their survey answers first, so you can refer to them ("you mentioned you watch mostly on TV…").
- Bring the note-taking template at the end of this file.

## How to ask
- **Ask about real past events, not hypotheticals.** "Tell me about the last time…" gets what people actually do.
  "Would you use…?" gets polite yeses.
- **Don't pitch SceneSeen until part 4.** Describing it early makes people agree with you.
- **Follow up with "why?", "what happened next?", "can you show me?"** The best detail comes from the second
  question, not the first.
- **Stay quiet after answers.** People often add the most useful part after a pause.
- **Don't correct or defend.** If they dislike an idea, ask why and write it down.
- If they have it, ask them to show you on their phone: a screenshot they saved, a comment they posted, a search.

## Part 1: Warm-up (2–3 minutes)
1. What have you been watching lately?
2. Where and how do you usually watch: TV, laptop, phone, clips? Does it change during Ramadan?
3. Who do you usually watch with?

## Part 2: The last time they liked something on screen (8–10 minutes)
The core of the interview. Get one or two specific stories in detail.

4. Think of the last time you noticed something in a series or film you liked: clothes, shoes, a bag, furniture, a
   place. What was it, and what were you watching?
   - What made you notice it? The character, the scene, the item itself?
5. What did you do in that moment?
   - Did you pause? Take a screenshot or a photo? Search right away, or later?
   - *If they screenshotted on a streaming app:* did the screenshot work, or was it black?
6. Walk me through what happened next, step by step.
   - Where did you search? What did you type or upload?
   - Did you check the comments, or ask anyone?
7. Did you find it?
   - *If yes:* exact or similar? How long did it take? Did you buy it, or anything like it?
   - *If no:* when did you give up? How did that feel?
8. How often does this happen? Is it usually clothes, or other things too?

## Part 3: Shopping and similar items (4–5 minutes)
9. When you can't find the exact item, would something similar be good enough? When yes, when no?
10. If the exact one is expensive, what do you do?
11. Which Egyptian brands do you buy from? Where do you find them: Instagram, stores, friends?

## Part 4: SceneSeen (6–8 minutes)
Read this out:
> We're building an app called SceneSeen. While you're watching, you point your phone's camera at the screen, and it
> shows you the clothes in the scene, with links to the same or similar items from Egyptian brands.

12. What's your first reaction? When would you use it, and when wouldn't you?
13. Would pointing your phone at the screen feel natural, or awkward? What if you're watching on your phone?
14. While it's working, the app asks which show and episode it is. Would you answer, or skip?
15. If the app couldn't find the exact item, would you:
    - tap a button to ask for it?
    - answer other people's questions if you knew where something was from?
    - vote on other people's answers?
    Why, or why not?
16. Results would have badges: **Verified** (confirmed by the stylist or brand), **Community pick** (voted by users)
    and **Similar** (suggested by the app). Would that change what you click or trust?
17. Some shows could put a QR code on screen instead. How would that feel while you're watching?

## Part 5: Media and cinema students only (3–5 minutes)
Skip for other participants.
18. In the productions you've worked on or studied, who chooses the costumes, and where do they come from?
    Bought, borrowed from brands, made, the actors' own?
19. Do costume designers or stylists get credit that viewers see? Would they want more?
20. Would you be interested in helping us rate how similar products are to what's on screen, or in answering
    viewers' questions in the app?
21. Do you know anyone in costume, styling or production who'd be open to a short chat? *(Only if the conversation
    went well; no pressure.)*

## Wrap-up (1–2 minutes)
22. Is there anything I should have asked but didn't?
23. Remind them of anything they opted into (beta testing, updates), and confirm it's still fine.
24. Thank them.

## After all interviews
- Put every note into one table and look for patterns: how many watch on TV, how many found what they looked for,
  how many would answer or vote.
- Write up 5–8 findings, each with a count ("7 of 10…") and one quote.
- Mark each SRS assumption from the README as **confirmed**, **partly confirmed** or **wrong**, and update the SRS
  if any are wrong.
